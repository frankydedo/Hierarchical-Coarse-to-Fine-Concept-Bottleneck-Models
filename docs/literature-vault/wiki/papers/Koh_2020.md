# Concept Bottleneck Models

## Summary
This seminal paper formalizes [[Concept Bottleneck Models]] (CBMs), an explainable-by-design paradigm where deep neural networks first predict an intermediate layer of predefined, human-understandable concepts $c \in \mathbb{R}^k$ from raw inputs $x$, and then predict the target output $y$ strictly from these concepts ($x \xrightarrow{g} c \xrightarrow{f} y$). Evaluating on knee osteoarthritis x-ray grading (OAI) and bird species identification (CUB), the authors demonstrate that CBMs achieve competitive task accuracy compared to standard end-to-end black-box models while enabling direct interpretability and [[Test-Time Intervention]]—the ability for human experts to inspect and edit concept predictions at inference time to rectify downstream mistakes.

## Key Contributions
- **Formalization of Concept Bottleneck Models:** Defines models of the form $\hat{y} = f(g(x))$ where prediction relies on the input exclusively through an intermediate bottleneck $\hat{c} = g(x)$ aligned component-wise with human concepts.
- **Three Training Paradigms:**
  - *Independent Bottleneck:* Learns $\hat{g}(x)$ and $\hat{f}(c)$ separately; $f$ is trained directly on ground-truth concepts $c$, but evaluated on $\hat{g}(x)$ at test time.
  - *Sequential Bottleneck:* Trains $\hat{g}(x)$ first on $(x, c)$, then trains $\hat{f}$ on the predicted concepts $(\hat{g}(x), y)$.
  - *Joint Bottleneck:* Minimizes a joint objective balancing task prediction loss and concept prediction loss: $\mathcal{L}_Y(f(g(x)), y) + \lambda \sum_j \mathcal{L}_{C_j}(g_j(x), c_j)$.
- **[[Test-Time Intervention]]:** Demonstrates that human-in-the-loop interaction—replacing inaccurate predicted concepts with true values at test time—substantially boosts downstream task accuracy, effectively allowing human practitioners and AI to collaborate productively.
- **Post-hoc Probing vs. Bottleneck Models:** Shows that post-hoc linear probes (e.g., TCAV, neuron concept probes) on standard models exhibit higher concept error and cannot reliably support interventions, highlighting the necessity of by-design bottleneck architectures.
- **Robustness to Spurious Background Shifts:** Using the TravelingBirds benchmark, demonstrates that CBMs are more robust to spurious train-test distribution shifts (e.g., backgrounds correlated with bird species) when concept-label relationships remain invariant across domains.

## Insights
- **Competitive Accuracy Without Black-Box Obscurity:** Restricting information flow through a human-interpretable concept bottleneck does not inherently sacrifice predictive performance compared to unconstrained end-to-end models on real-world clinical and vision benchmarks.
- **Intervention Trade-offs:** The independent training paradigm is most responsive to full concept interventions at test time because $f$ was trained on ground-truth concepts $c$; conversely, joint models perform slightly better without intervention but can suffer from concept misalignment or semantic drift when $\lambda$ is small.
- **Foundational Impact and Limitations:** While Koh et al. established flat concept bottlenecks, the requirement for dense, instance-level concept annotations and the lack of hierarchical structure directly inspired subsequent works on label-free CBMs ([[Label-Free Concept Bottleneck Model (LF-CBM)|LF-CBM]]), hierarchical variants ([[Hierarchical Concept Bottleneck Models]]), and methods to prevent concept leakage ([[Concept Leakage]], [[SUPCBM]], [[Hi-CBM]]).
