# ProtoEHR: Hierarchical Prototype Learning for EHR-based Healthcare Predictions

## Summary
The paper introduces ProtoEHR, an interpretable framework for healthcare predictions using [[Electronic Health Records (EHR)]]. Existing studies often ignore the intrinsic similarities within different levels of EHR data (codes, visits, patients). ProtoEHR leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) to automatically construct [[Medical Knowledge Graphs]] from medical codes, then applies a hierarchical representation learning approach combined with [[Prototype Learning]] at three levels: code, visit, and patient. This method captures both the hierarchical dependencies across levels and the intrinsic similarities within each level.

## Key Contributions
- **ProtoEHR Framework:** Integrates [[Prototype Learning]] with hierarchical representation learning to model similarities and hierarchies in EHR data.
- **LLM-based KG Construction:** Uses a combination of open-source and closed-source LLMs to retrieve, clean, and refine a medical knowledge graph from EHR codes, avoiding the high cost of pure GPT-4 construction.
- **Hierarchical Prototype Fusion:** Fuses representations at the code, visit, and patient levels using cross-attention with prototypes, providing transparency on which level (and which prototype) contributes most to a specific prediction.
- **Strong Performance:** Outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV across five tasks (mortality, readmission, length of stay, drug recommendation, phenotype prediction).

## Insights
- Patient-level prototypes are critical for patient-level tasks (e.g., mortality prediction), whereas visit-level information strongly influences code-level tasks like phenotype prediction.
- Interpretable AI in healthcare can be achieved by analyzing the attention weights of the hierarchical prototypes, allowing clinicians to trace predictions back to specific patient cohorts or medical codes.
